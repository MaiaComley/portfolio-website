03 Homepage

Homepage section order

The homepage does one job: convince someone to keep reading. Each section earns the next. Nothing is decorative.

  • 1 —Hero Name, single positioning line, one call to action. Not a slideshow. Not a video. One deliberate statement.
  • 2 —Introduction Two to three sentences. Who you are, what you care about, what kind of work you do. Written in first person. Not a list of adjectives.
  • 3 —Selected Work Two or three featured projects with skill area label, short outcome, and link. Not the full portfolio — a curated entry point.
  • 4 —Skills Grouped by discipline (strategy, content, digital, tools). Scannable. Not a tag cloud — structured and deliberate.
  • 5 —Latest Thoughts Two or three recent posts from the Thoughts section. Shows intellectual curiosity and range. Links through to the full section.
  • 6 —Contact Email and LinkedIn. Short. Confident. Optional: availability status ("Open to new roles" or "Currently at IDGateway").

06 Thoughts

Thoughts section structure

This is not a blog. It is a curated record of how you think — shorter and more considered than a blog post, longer and more substantive than a social caption. Think of it as observed notes.

It signals intellectual range, genuine interests, and a point of view — all things that distinguish a strategic marketer from someone who just executes.

Branding
Food marketing
Nutrition
Wellness
Consumer behaviour
Travel & culture
Design
Digital trends
  • Format —Short observation 200–400 words. A single observation, not a full essay. Could be triggered by a campaign you saw, a product, a restaurant, a trend.
  • Format —Analysis 500–800 words. A topic explored with a point of view. Branding of a food category, a campaign that worked and why, a shift in consumer behaviour.
  • Cadence —Quality over frequency Two to four pieces is better than twelve rushed ones. Start with one strong piece before launching the section.

Note Positioning

Suggested hero positioning line

Replace "Creative marketer blending strategy, content & design" with something more specific. A few options to consider:

  • "Marketing executive with a focus on brand, content, and digital — currently at IDGateway."
  • "I help brands find clarity — through content, strategy, and considered digital presence."
  • "Junior marketing executive. Interested in brand, food, wellness, and how people decide what they want."

The third option is most distinctive — it names real interests, which is rarer and more memorable than a list of services.
