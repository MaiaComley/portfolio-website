Task 01
Website Audit
Reviewed as a Creative Director hiring for AE, ME or Brand Executive roles
01
Critical

You lead with student work, not professional experience

The first thing a hiring manager sees is project-based work from university modules. PE Kit, Nature Valley, Simple Skincare — these read as briefs, not professional achievements. A brand executive role wants evidence of real-world output, not academic exercises.

02
Critical

No clear professional positioning

"Creative marketer blending strategy, content & design" is a placeholder, not a position. It tells me nothing about what you actually do, where you've worked, or what kind of role you're pursuing. A hiring director needs to know within 5 seconds.

03
Critical

IDGateway — your most relevant experience — is invisible

You're currently working in a marketing executive role managing SEO, website content, and campaigns. This is exactly what recruiters are looking for and it doesn't appear anywhere on the site. This is the single biggest gap.

04
Critical

No skills section

Recruiters scan for specific capabilities: SEO, content strategy, CMS, campaign planning. There's nowhere on the current site that maps your skills. This means the site can't pass an ATS-style skim or justify a conversation with a hiring manager.

05
Important

Projects aren't framed by outcome

Each project has a one-line description but no outcome, result, or strategic context. "Creating an integrated campaign" tells me what you did. "Grew engagement by X" or "positioned the brand as Y" tells me what you achieved. Outcomes are what get interviews.

06
Important

Single-page site limits depth and navigation

Everything lives on one scroll. There's no Experience page, no way to go deeper on a project, no Thoughts section. This forces all content to compete for the same space and prevents you from telling a layered professional story.

07
Important

Noirgaze is undersold

3k+ follower growth for a fashion start-up is a strong, concrete result. It's buried in a list with no context about what you actually did to achieve it — strategy, content format, cadence, platform. This could be your strongest case study.

08
Important

No contact beyond an email address

A hiring manager wants LinkedIn. Some will want a CV download. Email alone is a friction point — it requires someone to initiate a blank email rather than clicking a profile they can scan before deciding to reach out.

09
Refine

Visual identity doesn't yet match professional ambition

The current pink palette and project-grid layout reads closer to a creative student portfolio than the portfolio of a strategically-minded marketing executive. The aesthetic needs to mature to match the roles you're targeting — not more corporate, but more intentional.

10
Refine

No personality or intellectual range on show

The best marketing candidates show taste, curiosity, and a genuine point of view. The current site shows work but reveals nothing about how you think — about brands, culture, food, wellness, or consumer behaviour. A Thoughts section would close this gap immediately.

Task 04
Design Review
Typography, layout, spacing and visual hierarchy recommendations
Typography
Current: Bodoni Moda + Inter Tight

Keep the serif/sans pairing — but use Bodoni with more restraint

Bodoni Moda is a strong editorial face. The issue is weight — it's being used at the same size across too many elements. Reserve it for the name and section headlines only. Let Inter Tight carry all body and label copy. This gives Bodoni the contrast it needs to feel deliberate.

Type scale
Establish a clear scale

Set and lock a 5-step type scale

Hero name: 10–14rem · Section title: 2rem · Body: 1rem · Label/mono: 0.68rem · Caption: 0.72rem. Currently sizes feel inconsistent across sections — a locked scale makes the whole page feel more considered.

Colour
New palette: EEC643 / 5D2A42 / FB6376 / FCB1A6

Palette is now set — apply with discipline

Blackberry (#5D2A42) as ink gives all text warmth and personality. Yellow (#EEC643) is the dominant accent — use it in one hero block and one highlight only. Bubblegum Pink (#FB6376) is the secondary accent — skill dots, hover states, small moments. Blush (#FCB1A6) is a surface, not an accent.

Layout
Split hero

Split hero with photo is the right call — commit to it

The TheRed-style split gives the homepage immediate visual authority. The photo panel should be at least 45% of the viewport width. If you don't have a strong portrait yet, get one — it's the single biggest upgrade you can make to the hero.

Spacing
Negative space

The design earns its chic through what it leaves out

Section padding should be generous — 80–100px top and bottom. Between the hero and the intro, consider even more breathing room. The instinct to fill space is the enemy of the aesthetic you're going for. Trust the whitespace.

Visual hierarchy
Monospace labels

Use the mono labels as a structural system, not decoration

The ui-monospace label style (small, uppercase, tracked) is doing good work as a structural device. Apply it consistently: section eyebrows, card categories, nav items, footer. Avoid using it decoratively — every instance should be labelling something real.

Motion
Minimal animation

Keep animation to two moments: hero load and hover states

The name rise-up animation on page load is the right amount of personality. Hover states on work cards (scale + arrow reveal) add interactivity without noise. Don't add scroll-triggered animations to every section — it quickly reads as AI-template energy.

Mobile
Stack gracefully

Split hero stacks to photo-above, yellow-below on mobile

The hero photo should still appear on mobile — just stacked above the yellow content block. The name should remain large (clamp to ~4.5rem minimum). Skills grid collapses to 2 columns. Thoughts list hides the date column. All of this is already in the template.
